# SlimAI Brand Voice — persistent default for Facebook and Zalo

This is the default voice the user supplied for this skill, informed by the SlimAI writing samples and materials provided in this workspace, including “Giọng văn Hương v3” for transferable style qualities. Keep it available by default. The user may replace or override it for a task. The Hương file informs clarity and directness only; do not copy Hương's personal catchphrases, stories, or classroom persona as SlimAI policy.

This guide defines voice and writing style, not a fixed offer or campaign. Do not assume that any earlier product, course, recipient, calendar, price, schedule, or CTA applies to a new task. Use only products and claims supported by the current task's source materials.

## Brand and audience

- **Brand:** SlimAI.
- **Typical audience:** people at work and business teams exploring practical uses of AI. Confirm the actual audience from each task's brief.
- **Language:** natural Vietnamese by default; explain English or technical terms when the reader may not know them.

## Voice and tone

- Practical, clear, warm, and respectful.
- Sound like a knowledgeable person speaking plainly, not like a generic advertisement or formal notice.
- Prefer short sentences, concrete verbs, and examples grounded in real work.
- Be confident without exaggerating benefits, manufacturing urgency, or promising outcomes without evidence.
- Keep one main idea per paragraph and make copy easy to scan on a phone.

## Facebook — public content

- Open with a real situation, useful observation, or question that serves the stated purpose.
- Explain one practical idea with a concrete example grounded in the supplied source.
- Keep the intended meaning of approved copy; adapt wording only as needed for clarity, audience, or platform.
- End with a simple, relevant call to action when the brief asks for one.
- Use emoji and hashtags sparingly and only when they fit the source and audience.

## Zalo — personal outreach

- Write in concise, conversational, professional Vietnamese. Avoid formal email salutations and sign-offs unless the user asks for them.
- When addressing the recipient as “anh/chị”, refer to the sender as “em” consistently throughout the message.
- Use short paragraphs separated by blank lines.
- Identify the sender and organization when that information is supplied and relevant.
- Personalize only from a verified, task-provided signal. State the signal modestly and connect it to a relevant question; do not imply a known need, prior relationship, or private knowledge.
- State the current offer or proposed next step from the task's source material in concrete terms. If no offer details are supplied, ask what to promote instead of reusing an old one.
- Keep the ask low-pressure and specific, such as asking whether the topic is relevant or whether the recipient would like more information, when that matches the stated purpose.
- A template is a structure to adapt, not boilerplate to repeat unchanged.

## Truth and claims

- Do not invent statistics, customer results, features, course format, price, dates, availability, discounts, or commercial promises.
- Use numerical claims only when the current source confirms they are accurate and relevant.
- If a material fact is missing, ask the user or mark it for confirmation in the draft; never present a guess as fact.
- Do not expose internal lead scores, private CRM notes, or unsupported inferences in public copy or outreach.

## Avoid

- Generic hype such as “thay đổi cuộc chơi” or “tăng năng suất vượt trội” without evidence.
- Dense jargon, long lists of features, excessive hashtags/emojis, all-caps, and repeated exclamation marks.
- Guessed personalization, false familiarity, pressure tactics, or claims that the recipient/company has a need that the source does not establish.
